# From brief to visual direction

Use for a new design or an authorized visual overhaul. An existing brand or exact reference takes precedence; small fixes do not need a new concept.

## Make a direction concrete

Write a short internal direction connecting evidence to decisions: who is using this, what they need to do, how the product should feel, and how composition, type, color, and imagery express that. Replace adjectives such as “premium” with visible choices and a reason. For example: “A repair coordinator scans many jobs repeatedly; use aligned rows, clear status text, compact controls, and a persistent detail panel. Reserve the accent for the next action.”

For an open brief, consider two plausible approaches internally and select the better fit. This is a decision aid, not a demand to generate multiple sites or seek approval. Preserve useful asymmetry, centered layouts, expressive type, or familiar patterns when the content supports them.

| Content or task | Useful starting decision | Check before committing |
|---|---|---|
| One short offer and one action | Centered or compact composition | Does the offer remain clear without an invented hero image? |
| Story with substantial reading | Editorial grid, controlled measure, deliberate section rhythm | Does the reading order survive mobile and zoom? |
| Many comparable records | Aligned table or list with stable columns | Can users compare and locate an item without opening every record? |
| Visual objects to choose between | Image-led grid with consistent crops and useful labels | Do images reveal differences that matter to the choice? |
| Action requiring context | List/detail or form/summary composition | Is the relevant context visible during the decision? |

Choose density from how often users return and how much they compare. Set type roles using real text: display, reading, label, and numeric where useful. Evaluate contrast, line length, long labels, and tabular numerals at actual size. A type pairing should distinguish roles; one family may be sufficient. Choose a restrained set of spacing and surface tokens, then adjust optical exceptions deliberately. Do not borrow a font, color, or rounded-card treatment simply because it appears in a showcase.

For expressive work, give one coherent visual idea room to develop: a distinctive illustration system, unusual editorial rhythm, or a useful interactive object. Extra effects should reinforce that idea. For utility work, distinctiveness can come from excellent density, type, and task organization.

## Resolve assets before dependent polish

Choose the visual medium alongside the motion direction. Use the [medium decision table](../../blend-scroll/references/motion-direction.md#choose-the-experience-then-the-medium) when deciding between an interactive object, generated image, supplied media, and code-native drawing. Choose the strongest expected composition and behavior, not the most elaborate renderer. Imagery can stay still while the surrounding interface responds with carefully chosen micro-interactions.

For each prominent asset, establish its purpose, subject, source/permission, aspect ratio, focal point, and mobile crop. Inspect it at the size it will appear, including any text overlay. Use supplied product captures for product evidence; clearly identify illustrative concepts. Generate original imagery when available and appropriate, without inventing product capabilities or endorsements.

If suitable material is unavailable, choose a complete alternative: an original explanatory SVG, a labeled illustrative diagram, or a composition led by typography and actual content. Change the layout to support the alternative. Do not leave a broken image, empty hero rectangle, or unrelated stock photograph as the final draft. Disclose missing essential product evidence to the requester; decoration cannot replace it.

Reserve dimensions and use responsive sizing. Keep meaningful descriptions in text, avoid embedding essential copy inside images, and verify crops, sharpness, contrast, and loading failure. An asset that looks attractive alone may still be wrong for the page.

## Review the direction in the render

Look at the page at reading size, then as a whole composition. Check where attention lands first, whether the main action is findable, and whether sections have purposeful variation without feeling unrelated. Compare desktop and mobile compositions. Revise the biggest mismatch between the intended direction and the rendered result before polishing small decoration.
