# Calibrating taste against a reference

Use for a new visual direction, a substantial redesign, or a request to improve taste. This is a comparison method, not a house style. The user's supplied brand and requested resemblance take priority over novelty for its own sake.

## Match the level of decision, not only the ingredients

The motivating references succeed at different things: Claude separates calm reading from small expressive details; Apple gives a carefully resolved subject enough room to carry the page; OpenClaw makes type, illustration, color, and play belong to one product world. The supplied recording makes a tiny part transformation feel complete without moving the surrounding card. Choosing cream, a serif, rounded rectangles, and an animated icon does not reproduce those relationships.

When a user asks for work like a reference, compare it at the same viewport and apparent scale. Identify the visible relationship to preserve: subject-to-page scale, heading-to-body contrast, reading measure, density, edge treatment, graphic vocabulary, or pacing. Implement that relationship with the user's content. Do not deliberately avoid a reference's serif, palette, or composition merely to claim originality when the user actually wants that direction. Keep copied branding and unauthorized assets out of an original identity.

## Resolve the large relationships first

Inspect the first viewport and a whole-page reduction before close-up decoration. Can you point to the intended first subject, the next reading step, and the primary action? Do sections change emphasis because their content changes, or repeat the same component recipe? Does the visual anchor still carry the design with animation paused?

Compare masses rather than counting components: a product image can occupy most of a view if it deserves inspection; a companion may need only a small corner. Whitespace creates grouping and attention, but unused space around weak content does not create quality. A background tint should define a meaningful region, not compensate for a missing composition.

For a multi-example request, compare the two pages side by side. Different business names over the same split hero, italic keyword, three pastel cards, and centered closing pitch do not establish range. Differences should follow the businesses' evidence, reading order, visual material, and actions. Related pages within one brand should remain consistent.

## Then judge the material up close

| Look at | Evidence of care | Common surface substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Purposeful weight, readable supporting text, natural wraps, optical alignment | A fashionable family with compressed spacing or decorative microtext |
| Artwork | Recognizable subject, coherent silhouette, material and crop, useful detail at final size | A huge generic flower, star, blob, or primitive with no role beyond filling the hero |
| Line illustration | Designed paths, shared stroke behavior, a stable anchor, clear moving parts | A stock icon tilted or bounced as one unit |
| Surfaces | Consistent borders, corners, layering, and hierarchy | More shadows, pills, gradients, or grain without a visual reason |
| Copy | A clear offer, an inspectable example, specific section purpose | Taglines in every eyebrow, invented proof, repeated “small things / big impact” claims |
| Motion | Observable change, recognition time, coherent return, quiet surroundings | Everywhere-on-entry reveals, global floating, or perpetual mascot acting |

These are diagnoses, not bans on any motif, component, or effect. A flower can be the right subject for a florist; a bounce can be the right response for a soft character. The question is whether the material and scale belong to this product and satisfy the requested reference.

## A useful correction is visible

Name the mismatch, the decision, and the evidence that will resolve it. For example: “The mascot occupies more space than the product demonstration. Reduce its role to a small companion beside the response, and give the response the dominant area.” Or: “The badge and its mark arrive together, so the result never resolves. Grow the badge first, reveal the mark from its anchor afterward, then hold.”

Re-render the affected composition and replay the affected sequence. Do not report taste as passed because a checker found no errors. If the output still relies on weak artwork, generic copy, or indistinct composition, revise that foundation before adding fine effects. Keep this diagnosis in working notes or a requested audit, not in the product's UI.
