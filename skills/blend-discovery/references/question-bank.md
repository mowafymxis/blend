# Design interview question bank

Select relevant questions, prefill established answers, and ask in rounds. The examples are vocabulary aids, not defaults or a compulsory menu. Ask for the user's own answer too. Keep each question answerable without knowing design software or animation libraries.

## 1. Product, scope, and taste

1. What is the product, who is it for, and what should someone do first?
2. Are we making a landing page, an app, a portfolio, or a connected set of pages? Which flows must work in the first draft?
3. What must stay from the current version, and what feels wrong? Point to composition, type, artwork, motion, density, or copy if possible.
4. Which two or three qualities should dominate: calm and intelligent, bold and precise, friendly and playful, experimental and artistic, or something else? Which quality should never appear?
5. For each reference, what do you want to carry over: the overall layout, type, theme, drawings, character, animation, or only the level of finish? How close should the resemblance be?
6. What content, brand assets, examples, and working integrations already exist? What may be invented as clearly labeled concept content?
7. Which outcome matters most in the first draft? What would make you reject it even if the code works?

## 2. Typography, theme, and composition

8. Should headings feel editorial with a serif, crisp with a sans-serif, playful, technical, or use an exact supplied font? Would you like real-text specimens before choosing?
9. Should body text and controls feel neutral, warm, compact, or spacious? Any font, italic treatment, all-caps labels, or tiny text you dislike?
10. Which languages are required? Is Arabic/RTL or mixed-script content needed? Are fonts restricted to existing or freely licensed assets?
11. Light only, dark only, or both? If both, what opens first: system preference or a specified theme, and should a manual choice persist?
12. Which surface direction fits: warm paper, neutral white, charcoal, near-black, colored fields, or supplied colors? What accent colors should be used or avoided?
13. Should the page feel open and spacious, balanced, or dense? Should the first view be led by typography, the product/demo, project work, a character, or a combination?
14. Do you want mostly open sections, framed cards, editorial columns, an image-led gallery, or a mixture driven by content? Any existing layout to preserve?
15. How should details feel: precise borders, softly rounded surfaces, flat ink, tactile paper, polished material, or another direction? Are grain, glow, gradients, and shadows welcome?

## 3. Illustration and assets

16. Do you want hand-drawn pencil/ink illustrations like the supplied small drawings, precise vector diagrams, photography, rendered objects, or a mix? Which role belongs to each?
17. For drawn art, should it be clean irregular monoline, textured graphite, chalk-like, or visibly sketchy? Mostly outline, a small filled accent, or broad color areas?
18. Where should drawings live: feature cards, empty states, section transitions, navigation details, or hero? How prominent should they be?
19. What should each drawing communicate? Prefer hands and tools, abstract structures, objects from the product, or another subject family?
20. Are original generated images/mockups welcome? Which assets must be exact? Is there time/budget to produce strong hero material?
21. Should assets change between light and dark themes, or retain the same color and composition?

## 4. Mascot branch — ask when desired or under consideration

22. Do you want a mascot? If yes, an existing character, an original relative of a visual style, or several small silhouette options to choose from?
23. What medium fits: stepped/pixel geometry like the mascot reference, drawn line art, flat filled vector, soft layered illustration, or 3D?
24. What personality should show: attentive, curious, mischievous, reassuring, calm, energetic? What would feel childish or annoying?
25. Should it be a tiny cameo, accompany the product demo, sit beside important actions, or lead the hero? Should it stay in one place or appear at selected moments?
26. Which reactions do you want: look toward input, blink, read, carry a prop, acknowledge a completed action, or a short welcome? Which real events should cause them?
27. How often should it move while idle: never, occasionally, or sustained ambient acting? Should people be able to pause or hide it?
28. Should interacting with it do anything useful, or is it decorative? Are sound, cursor following, or surprises wanted? Do not add these by assumption.

## 5. Motion — distinguish four separate choices

29. For small drawings, do you want internal transformations like the recording, stroke drawing, a subtle state change, or still illustrations?
30. What starts local motion: first viewport entry, hover and keyboard focus, a click/replay control, or an actual product event? Should it finish once or repeat?
31. What pacing fits: quick and precise, soft and relaxed, or playful with a little overshoot? Is the supplied clip the timing reference or only the drawing style?
32. Do you want ambient atmosphere like the moving text/particle field on OpenClaw? Where should it appear, how noticeable should it be, and how should it rest or pause?
33. Do you want a continuous scene that advances and reverses as you scroll, a short reveal when a section enters view, or ordinary scrolling with local interactions? These produce very different experiences.
34. If continuous scroll is selected, what should visibly transform and what is the payoff? One short signature scene or a longer sequence of chapters? Should part of the page remain pinned?
35. When you say “like a video while scrolling,” must the result use live drawn/3D objects, can it seek through an actual video, can it use rendered frames, or may the AI recommend the medium? Explain quality and loading tradeoffs before choosing.
36. On phones, should the big scene stay with a simpler composition, become a shorter sequence, or become still steps? Which experience matters most?
37. Do you want animated page transitions, link scribbles, moving tab indicators, hover image details, or only the selected illustrated scenes? Which effects should be absent?
38. Which areas must stay quiet for reading? When a large scene is active, should nearby illustrations and mascot activity pause?

## 6. Content, delivery, and first-draft priorities

39. What pages/sections and real proof should be present? Any copy to preserve verbatim? What is the writing voice?
40. What does the primary action actually do: navigate, start a local demo, download, submit an inquiry, or call an existing service?
41. Which browsers/devices, existing stack, asset-size constraints, and deadline matter? May we add dependencies if a selected behavior needs them?
42. Which three decisions should the first draft get especially right? Which may the AI choose, and which should remain open for you?

Accessible controls, honest status, and a usable reduced-motion alternative are execution requirements. Ask how the quieter composition should look, not whether to make essential content accessible. Once the relevant choices are settled, summarize and build without asking the same preferences again.
